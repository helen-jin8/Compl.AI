# Vision — Compl.AI

**The autonomous hardware compliance firm.** It scopes what certification a device
needs, routes the founder to a lab that will take them, and runs its entire
go-to-market with no employees. Human judgment is purchased by the task, through an
API, only where a machine genuinely cannot substitute.

## The wedge

A pre-launch hardware founder has to clear FCC and UL certification before they
can legally ship. The process is opaque and the people who understand it have all
moved upmarket. Email a compliance consultant about certifying a small device and
you get told they don't work with companies that size. That rejection is the whole
opportunity: thousands of funded campaigns publish a ship date and a device with a
radio or a battery, never file with the FCC, and have no one to call.

We serve exactly the segment the incumbents reject. We start where they say no.

## Why it can exist now

The scoping work that justified a $12k consultant decomposes into three layers,
and the machine handles the first two on its own:

- **Perception** — does this thing have a battery, a radio, a ship date, a child
  audience? A model reads the page and quotes the exact line that justifies each
  attribute, so the read is auditable without trusting the model. Reading a product
  page is not expert work.
- **Rules** — which standards those attributes trigger. A deterministic, auditable
  lookup table. You can read it. No model decides it.
- **Authority** — is the scope right and what does it cost? That comes from
  accredited labs, whose accreditation is the thing legally on the line, and from a
  named expert who reviews and signs the finished report. No consultant holds the
  labs' authority anyway.

The expensive part was never the knowledge. It was the labor of applying it to one
specific device. That labor is now a model call plus a lookup. A person enters
once, at the end, to review and sign the deliverable — the one step where human
judgment actually changes the outcome. So the firm runs without a compliance
department, and prices the work where the rejected segment can afford it.

## The product is the message

The outbound email is a free sample of the deliverable. The prospecting agent and
the compliance agent are the same agent, so the first thing a founder receives is
the actual standards list for *their* device, with clauses cited, the lab that
covers it, the cost band, and the weeks. We don't pitch the service; we do a piece
of the work and send it. That is why it converts, and why it is not spam — spam
doesn't scope your device and invite you to correct it.

The go-to-market motion and the product are one artifact. That is the design.

## What we are, precisely

We scope and we route. We do not test and we do not certify — that authority sits
with accredited labs and always will. A report is *reviewed and signed by a named
expert*, never *certified*. Being precise about who holds what authority is the
entire trust argument, and we hold that line in every line of copy.

## The honest defaults

Two moments define the brand. Every attribute in a report cites the line on the
page it came from, so a founder can check our read instead of trusting it — and
when the expert review changes the draft, we show the before and after rather than
hide that the first pass missed something. When no expert can review a paid report
in time, we refund automatically and say so plainly — the same honesty the founder
was denied by the consultancy that wouldn't take them. Trust is the moat in a
category built on opacity. We win it by being the one party in the chain that tells
the truth about what it does and doesn't know.

## The arc

- **Year 1 — the wedge.** Autonomous scoping and outreach for pre-launch hardware
  founders the incumbents reject. Free scope, paid signed review, lab routing.
- **Year 2 — the network.** Every scoped device is qualified inbound for a lab.
  Labs pay for that. We become the routing layer between thousands of small
  hardware companies and a dozen labs that want the work.
- **Year 3 — up the chain.** Same structure — perception, rules, authority — over
  the next opaque, gatekept compliance regime. SOC 2, then whatever comes after.

The scoping layer is the wedge. The business is being the routing layer, run by an
agent, between a fragmented supply of small hardware companies and a concentrated
demand from labs. The company has no employees. It hires perception by the task.

## North star

**Qualified replies from real hardware founders per 100 sourced prospects.** It is
the one asset that cannot be manufactured, the top of every downstream funnel, and
the honest measure of whether an agent-run firm is actually creating value a person
would pay for — without a person in the path.
